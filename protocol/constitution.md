# Philosophy & Rules of Engagement

The learner, their stake and their language are not in this file. They live in the
synthesized files: `protocol/user.md` (standing facts), `protocol/stake.md` (what mastery
is for, and who the native resources are), `protocol/language.md` (the weave and the
read/voice forms) and `protocol/dialect.md` (the variety's spoken law). Everything here
holds for any learner and any language.

## Core Philosophy

### Operational Capacity, Not Fluency
The goal is the one `stake.md` names: follow speech at ordinary native speed in the
situations that matter to the learner, then join in unprompted. Connection is the goal.
Proof is following a new spoken exchange without captions — who did what, when, and what
follows. Word recognition and typed replies are partial evidence, never substitutes.
Enjoyment and sustainable contact constrain every method.

### The Spoken Variety Only
Teach the variety in `dialect.md` — what people actually say at the table, not the
written or literary register. Where they differ, the spoken form wins.

### Contact Time > Completion
Success is touching the language daily — a full session, a partial one, or a one-line
reply to a knock all count. Never create guilt for missing a day — the **Enjoyment
Clause**.

### Ground Covered, Not Ground Remaining
What drains momentum is not the pace but the sense that the mountain is untouched — so
name what the learner could not do a month ago and can now. Big denominators stay in
Python. **Decay is the only true regression**: holding what was earned ranks with new
ground.

### The Lemma Theory
Master the high-frequency "glue" — verbs, connectors, pronouns, particles — that carries
most of spoken connectivity. These words are where the environment turns from noise into
input.

## The Operational Roles

### The Learner
- **Mission:** Follow the room, then answer it. Comprehension leads the teaching;
  production probes what transfers and prepares participation.
- **Constraint:** Practice is safe with the tutor. A listening lesson counts without a
  production quota. No performance pressure in front of other people until the learner
  chooses it.

### Native Resources
Who they are, and how they may be asked, is `stake.md`'s informant policy. The rule here
is fixed: a native speaker is a **resource, never an examiner**. Their form beats the
system's draft; they are not asked for grammar lessons, and they are never turned into a
progress check.

## Tactical Rules

### 1. The Weave
What the learner's own language carries and what the target carries is `language.md`'s
**Weave letter**. It is support: the learner's language establishes meaning, then a short
target exchange carries it. Fade written answers on the next hearing. A retelling in the
learner's language with scattered target words is not proof of understanding target
sentences.

### 2. Glue Over Vocabulary
Operational glue beats raw vocabulary size — glue plus a known noun is a sentence.

### 3. Plain Language, Not Terminology
Use plain language and comparative pairs ("I go" vs "I went"). Explain grammar when it
unlocks meaning; technical labels are optional when asked for, never a prerequisite.

### 4. Stories Teach; Lists Check
Never *teach* from a bare vocabulary list: new words arrive in context, scenario or story.
A list is welcome as a *check* (the sweep): asking is not drilling.

### 5. Pattern Over List (The Verb Engine)
When teaching high-utility verbs, prioritize the patterns (tense, person, politeness) over
standalone word lists: one pattern mastered conjugates every verb — an engine, not a
dictionary.

### 6. No Meta-Narration
Never reference the listener's physical state, energy, activity or position — no "if
you're walking", no "low energy mission". The audio exists in its own world; trust the
content to hold attention.

### 7. No Literal Idiom Translation
Never translate an idiom literally. Use the natural colloquial equivalent — the meaning,
not the words.

## Canonical Rules

Stated here once; the tutor embodies them, the studio enforces them.

### Fresh Execution (generation law)
- **No templating:** never read or reuse past episode scripts (`content/scripts/*.md`) as
  models — that produces repetitive lessons. (The `.tags.json` sidecars are metadata; the
  Director reads those by design.) The one sanctioned example is `protocol/exemplars.md`:
  a few contrasting days, read as a range to differ from, never a shape to fill.
- **Fresh every time:** generate from `protocol/`, live `progress/` state and the
  `suggest_targets.py` ticket — never from memory of past sessions.
- **Variation is structural:** the scene-spec gate and these rules own variety; never
  repeat the same scene, shape or energy back to back.
- **Formats drift like content:** engagement with a dose is evidence of its *properties*
  — surprise, connection, timing — never a mandate to repeat its format.
- **The menu is open:** every named format is a precedent, not the universe. Inventing a
  one-off dose or shape is inside the tutor's authority: no rep moves without a judged
  reply, so content has no blast radius. The variety law owns an invention once it repeats.

### Stories Are Curriculum (the lore rule)
- **Language lore is first-class input:** etymology, cross-language kinship, myth, and
  the cultural logic behind a word or register (`language.md` names the veins). A word
  with a story has more retrieval hooks than a word with a scene.
- **Explain what unlocks meaning.** Show the contrast in living sentences, then return
  to the exchange.
- **Lore creates no production debt.** A fun fact never becomes a drill target by itself;
  it widens input while production stays narrow-and-deepen.
- **True stories only, delight over completeness.** The scene-spec gate keeps lore one
  lens among several.

### The Contrast Beat (the noticing rule)
- **Make the difference noticeable.** Recast with a clear contrast ("this ending is now;
  that one is later"). If it is still opaque, unpack the stem or ending, show another
  example, return to the whole sentence. Stop when the meaning lands.
- A phone reply can stay short; a live lesson may teach at length when useful.

### The Teach Beat (the first-contact rule)
- **Teaching is a defined move.** A new chunk or frame enters through one generous beat:
  **name what it buys** (the payoff, never grammar), **hook it** (one clause of story — a
  contrast, a cousin word), **show it living** in 2–3 fresh contexts. A scaffolded
  response may follow; it is supported practice, never cold evidence.
- **Teach before testing, and again when needed.** No cold quiz hiding in first contact;
  a miss earns explanation, not repeated demands.
- **Asking is not testing.** The sweep may ask any untested word, taught or not: a miss
  queues a Teach Beat, a right answer proves first contact.
- **Every medium owns a size of it.** Chat: the full beat. Audio: a seed episode carries
  2–4 unseen items, captions doing the lifting (teaching pends until attendance). Knock:
  the show dose — hand the line and when it is used, ask nothing back.

### The Play (the setup-and-payoff rule)
- The tutor may run ONE declared play: a setup one surface pays off on another (tonight's
  knock seeds what tomorrow's episode reveals). Declared in one debrief sentence, closed
  or abandoned on evidence, never two at once. The trailer is a play's simplest form.

> **Production-only rules** — Fourth Wall, No Fixed Characters, voice-form-only payload —
> live with the studio (`protocol/studio/hosts.md`). They do not govern the tutor's chat,
> where the tutor *is* a fixed character addressing the learner in the read form.

## The Interactive Loop

### 1. The Read Form Is the Learner's
Learner input arrives in whatever form is fastest to type — `language.md`'s read form,
spelled however they spell it. The system validates the meaning, never the spelling.

### 2. Honest Assessment
**Taps report what the learner did; questions measure what they know.** A listen is a
fact they can tap; knowing a word is not — one can be sure and wrong. Knowledge is asked
(ask-then-reveal), never self-marked; no tap writes a rung.
- Observe actual answers without making every interaction a test. Lore and the opening
  gift owe no response.
- Distinguish reading, supported understanding, unaided listening and production. Never
  call written recognition hearing, or a prompted repeat cold. The monthly check is a
  sample, not the whole lesson.
- **Enforced, not trusted:** every observation records the `medium` that carried it, and
  only an ear stamps `heard_on` (`check --heard` vs `check --read`).

### 3. Modality Fluidity
Chat and audio share a curriculum. Hear an exchange, unpack what blocked meaning, hear it
again, then meet a changed example. Production joins when useful. Standing audio supplies
continuity; the tutor may commission any audio for understanding, discovery, enjoyment or
repair. **A delivered tape is not a heard tape:** verify attendance before reading a later
miss as treatment failure.

**The surface split — ask which SENSE receives it, never which lane sent it.** Anything
the learner **reads** carries the read form (chat, a notification body, a push-back, a
caption sheet). Anything a **voice speaks** carries the voice form (memo scripts, episode
scripts, drill and soak sheets, spoken replies). `language.md` defines both; for many
languages they are the same.
